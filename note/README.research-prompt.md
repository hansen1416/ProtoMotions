# Deep Research Prompt: Morphology-Generalized Physics-Based Motion Imitation

## Context

I am training a single physics-based humanoid controller (PPO + imitation reward, IsaacGym, 69 DOF SMPL body) to imitate AMASS/HUMOS motions across 128 SMPL body shape variants (64 beta shapes × 2 genders). The beta parameters are uniformly distributed in `[-3, 3]^10`. The training data is 1024 motion clips × 128 shape variants = 131,072 paired (motion, body shape) trajectories, generated by HUMOS. Each environment is assigned one fixed SMPL body shape (XML asset), and samples motions only from the matching-shape bucket.

**Observation:** `[max_coords_obs (~400 dim), mimic_target_poses (~576 dim), previous_actions (69 dim), morphology_obs (11 dim)]`. The 11-dim morphology vector is `[gender_id, beta_1/3, ..., beta_10/3]`.

**Results so far:**

| Architecture | Description | Reward after 3 days (4×A40, 4096 envs) |
|---|---|---|
| Single-motion baseline | Fixed shape, fixed motion clip | >0.95 |
| MLP + raw concat | 11-dim morphology directly in obs | ~0.85 |
| MLP + shape embed | 11-dim → Linear(64) → SiLU → concat | ~0.85 (nearly identical to concat) |
| FiLM | 11-dim → conditioner → gamma/beta per trunk layer | ~0.72 (failed to learn) |

FiLM failed due to two compounding issues: (1) fanout bottleneck — a 64-unit conditioner must produce `2 × 6 × 1024 = 12,288` scale/shift outputs, diluting gradients; (2) multiplicative instability — trunk gradients at layer `l` are scaled by `gamma_l`, which becomes noisy and shape-dependent when the conditioner trains slowly.

The ~0.85 ceiling is partially explained by failures on **squat and crawl motions** (ground contact in non-standing configurations). Locomotion-class motions converge well. The 0.95+ single-motion baseline confirms the architecture can handle individual motions well — the difficulty is in jointly learning across 1024 diverse clips and 128 body shapes.

**Critically, concat and shape-embed perform identically.** Adding a nonlinear projection layer before concatenation provided no benefit. This is the key empirical finding that needs explanation and guides what to try next.

## Research Questions

### 1. Why are concat and shape-embed identical in performance?

Three competing hypotheses need to be evaluated against the literature and theory:
- (a) The network can already use raw betas effectively — encoding capacity is not the bottleneck; the limit is motion difficulty (squat/crawl failures)
- (b) The shape signal is partially redundant — proprioceptive states (joint angles, velocities, root height) already implicitly encode body shape to some degree, so additional explicit encoding adds marginal value
- (c) The conditioning signal is dominated by the motion distribution — with 1024 diverse clips, the inter-motion variance swamps the inter-shape variance in the gradient signal, making the shape branch invisible to the optimizer

What does the literature say about when explicit morphology conditioning does vs does not help? Are there empirical results in modular robot control, sim-to-real morphology transfer, or SMPL physics controllers that shed light on this?

### 2. What alternative conditioning architectures should be explored?

Beyond FiLM and concat, what conditioning mechanisms have been shown to work well when:
- The conditioning signal (11 dims) is much lower-dimensional than the main obs (1000+ dims)
- The conditioning signal correlates with continuous physical properties (mass, limb length), not discrete classes
- Training is online RL, not supervised (no direct supervision signal on the conditioning pathway)

Specific candidates to evaluate:
- **Hypernetwork / weight generation**: conditioning network generates a subset of actor weights (biases only, or a low-rank adaptation) from the shape vector. Analogous to LoRA.
- **Physics-derived features**: instead of raw betas, compute and provide `[total_mass, com_height, limb_lengths (×5), limb_mass_ratios (×5)]` from the SMPL kinematic model. These are continuous, physically interpretable, and may generalize better to unseen shapes than raw PCA components.
- **Asymmetric conditioning**: condition only the critic (value function) on shape, not the actor. The critic uses shape to predict expected return (shape-aware baseline), while the actor receives shape via the standard obs. Does this help training stability compared to conditioning both?
- **Per-shape running normalization**: maintain separate observation running mean/std per shape bucket, so the trunk always sees normalized inputs regardless of shape. Simple, but addresses the concern that raw betas shift the obs distribution.
- **Morphology-adaptive initialization**: initialize each episode from a shape-specific standing reference pose rather than the reference motion frame. This provides an implicit curriculum signal without changing the architecture.

### 3. What is the state of the art for morphology-generalized control?

Survey recent work (2020–2025) on:
- **Morphology-agnostic/universal robot controllers**: Metamorph (Gupta et al.), One Policy to Control Them All (Huang et al.), AMORPHEUS, Graph Neural Network policies for variable morphology — do any of these work on humanoid-class complexity? What conditioning mechanisms do they use?
- **SMPL-based physics controllers at scale**: PHC (Luo et al. 2023), PULSE, Universal Humanoid Motion Representations — do any of these train across body shapes, and if so how do they condition on shape?
- **Sim-to-real with morphology mismatch**: domain randomization on robot physical parameters (mass, inertia, link lengths) — what conditioning strategies are used, and do they generalize?
- **FiLM at scale**: are there RL settings where FiLM conditioning has been shown to work robustly? What fixes the fanout/instability issues (gradient clipping, conditioner warmup, initialization of gamma near 1.0)?
- **Hypernetworks in RL**: are there examples where hypernetworks successfully condition a policy on a low-dimensional context vector, particularly in physics simulation?

### 4. What training-side interventions could push past the 0.85 ceiling?

The squat/crawl failures suggest the 0.85 ceiling is partially a motion-difficulty problem, not purely a shape-conditioning problem. Survey:
- **Residual PD control**: `q_target = q_ref + scale · action` instead of `q_target = q_neutral + scale · action`. PHC uses this. Does it help on contact-rich motions?
- **Per-motion curriculum and adaptive sampling**: weight motions by inverse success rate. PHC's progressive training (PMCP) added motions incrementally. Is there a continuous-weight equivalent that works without progressive restarts?
- **Reference state initialization (RSI) variants**: early-termination + RSI is standard. Are there variants specifically designed for ground-contact motions (squat, crawl, sit) where the reference pose is near the floor?
- **Contact-aware reward**: PHC uses a contact reward term. AMP does not. Is contact reward necessary for squat/crawl convergence, or does pure pose tracking suffice given enough training?
- **Phase-based gating**: some motions have discrete phases (stand → squat → stand). Should the policy observe a phase variable derived from the reference motion?

### 5. What are the most interesting novel research directions?

Given the empirical finding that concat ≈ shape-embed, the paper's contribution needs to be reframed or extended. Suggest:
- What research angles in this space have not been explored in the literature and would be most impactful for ICRA 2027?
- Is there a stronger version of the "shape generalization" claim — e.g., zero-shot transfer to unseen body shapes, or shape-conditioned motion retargeting (where the policy implicitly adapts stride length, timing, balance strategy to body proportions)?
- The embodiment encoding probe (fitting a linear regression from policy activations to physical body properties) — is this a known analysis technique in the literature, and are there stronger variants that would be more convincing to reviewers?
- What failure modes of the current approach (fixed shape per env, no cross-shape transfer during training) would a stronger method need to address?

\section{Evaluation Protocol}\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Populations and body-matched replay}\label{sec:evaluation_protocol}

Each reference is rolled out only in an environment using its exact asset
identity, so a reference is never assigned to a different body. Evaluation
uses the mean action and reference initialization, disables training
termination, and records errors over the valid reference horizon, capped at 600
control steps (20\,s at 30\,Hz); success therefore refers to the evaluated
horizon, not necessarily the whole source clip. Routine monitoring and the
held-out evaluation select one body variant per clip with a deterministic hash
of the clip identity (Appendix~\ref{app:reproducibility}), so the clip list is
fixed while the body panel rotates across scheduled evaluations. Validation has
been monitored repeatedly and is not an untouched test set; the clip-disjoint
test manifest stays outside training and model selection and is evaluated once
(Section~\ref{sec:test_results}).

The development corpus contains 150 clips and all 128 body configurations
(19,200 variants), reserved within the full training split; it is not a
held-out generalization benchmark. Because compute was limited, architecture
and reference-processing choices are investigated on this subset with a fixed
simulation budget, whereas the full-scale run trains on the 18,855 training
clips (Table~\ref{tab:dataset_counts}). Table~\ref{tab:evidence_populations}
lists the populations used. Held-out identity is clip-grouped, not a
demonstrated subject or recording split.

\paragraph{Unseen-body construction and replay.}
Each unseen panel contains 64 new coefficient vectors instantiated on male and
female SMPL templates. Inside vectors lie in $[-3,3]^{10}$; outside vectors
have $\|\beta\|_\infty\in(3,5]$, with 16 vectors (32 bodies) in each of four
half-unit tiers. Inside denotes membership of the training coefficient box, not
of the convex hull of trained bodies. Both panels use matching HUMOS references
and assets, frame-zero offsets, and full reference refinement; the build report
records zero penetration under its refinement diagnostic but does not certify
dynamic feasibility. Replay uses mean actions, reference initialization, 32
environments per asset, and at most 210 control steps (7\,s), with success as
in Eq.~\eqref{eq:success}. The 233 training-split identities isolate the body
axis, while the other 23 validation/test identities remain separate. The
trained-body control uses a different 150-clip panel and refinement run, so only
within-dataset checkpoint deltas are paired comparisons.

\paragraph{Checkpoints and uncertainty.}
The original \texttt{refined} checkpoint is epoch 28,170 (distinct from the
epoch-28,159 monitoring snapshot of Appendix~\ref{app:history}); the
prespecified \texttt{fulldata} candidate is epoch 34,000 and trained on all
original motion splits. Both are frozen for replay, and duration and motion
coverage change together, preventing causal attribution. Each dataset has two
original-checkpoint runs and one candidate run. Paired 95\% bootstrap intervals
resample clips with the body panel fixed; they do not resample training seeds
or unseen bodies. A change is called beyond noise only if its interval excludes
zero and its magnitude exceeds the original-checkpoint rerun delta, so without
a checkpoint ladder this measures simulator variability only. Per-body
intervals are exploratory and unadjusted for multiple testing. On all 256
clips, identical-checkpoint success labels agree on 99.33\% of inside and
98.68\% of outside pairs, versus 99.96\% in the control. Pooled success differs
by at most 0.05 pp, but individual pairs can change by metres in peak error, so
small pooled changes coexist with local instability.

\subsection{Metrics}\label{sec:metrics}

For each evaluated clip/shape pair $i$, the per-frame mean body position error is
\begin{equation}
    d_{i,t} = \frac{1}{B}\sum_{j=1}^{B}
                 \|p_{i,t,j}-\hat{p}_{i,t,j}\|_2 ,
\end{equation}
and the configured success indicator is
\begin{equation}
    S_i = \mathbf{1}\!\left[
             \max_{t<T_i}d_{i,t}\leq 0.5\ \mathrm{m}
          \right],
    \label{eq:success}
\end{equation}
where $T_i$ is the evaluated horizon. This threshold is deliberately distinct
from the smoother training reward, and passing it does not imply fine-grained
pose accuracy or biomechanical realism. We also report time-averaged mean
body-position error, mean global body-rotation geodesic error (not a local
joint-angle error), the time average of the largest body-position error within
each frame (the logged \emph{mean maximum-joint error}), and the windowed
normalized jerk of Appendix~\ref{app:smoothness}, whose high-jerk statistic is
the percentage of windows in which any body exceeds 6,500. Errors are averaged
over valid frames per motion and then over motions, including failed rollouts;
offline comparisons aggregate saved per-motion records over unique clip/shape
pairs.

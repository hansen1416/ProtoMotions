\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}
Physics-based character animation should reproduce actions on digital humans
with different proportions and dynamics, and a shared controller offers a way
to reuse one motion repertoire across those bodies, provided that references
and simulated assets stay consistent. Changes in limb lengths, mass
distribution, and collision geometry alter both the reference trajectory and the
dynamics required to follow it. Learning across body shapes requires a
consistent relationship between source motion, shape-specific reference, and
simulated body. Good tracking of new motions on a
familiar body does not establish transfer to a new body, and a high average
over bodies can conceal severe failures.

The building blocks exist: large motion resources, shape-conditioned reference
generation, and scalable imitation~\cite{amass,humanml3d,humos,phc,protomotions}.
Body-conditioned control is established~\cite{won2019}, but the large-scale
trackers Perpetual Humanoid Control (PHC) and Adversarial Skill Embeddings
(ASE) learn from motion collections and report results on a single body shape;
PHC supports body variation, although its quantitative evaluations use the mean
body of the Skinned Multi-Person Linear model (SMPL)~\cite{phc,ase,smpl}
(Section~\ref{sec:related_work}). What remains open is how reliably one
controller learns and transfers across a large corpus of explicitly paired
motions and bodies.

We approach it with MorphMimic: a paired corpus in which every clip/body
identity is preserved from HUMOS generation through simulation and replay
(20,951 clip identities and 128 body configurations), and one shared temporal
slot/type-attention controller that trains directly on 18,855 of those clips
using streamed motion residency (Figure~\ref{fig:trained_multibody_demo}).
Evaluation separates motion exposure from body exposure. The contributions are
the paired corpus and workflow, the shared controller with streamed residency,
exposure-separated evaluation of trained and unseen bodies, and three-seed
network comparisons with a reference-refinement audit. We inherit the simulator
integration, proximal policy optimization (PPO), and motion-tracking
infrastructure from ProtoMotions and do not claim them, or morphology
conditioning itself, as new. HumanML3D captions are metadata, not policy
inputs: MorphMimic is reference-conditioned imitation, not text-conditioned
control.

The original MorphMimic checkpoint achieves 99.14\% success on 1,048
clip-disjoint test clips, each paired with one selected trained body. On 233
training-split clips across 128 unseen inside-range bodies and 128 unseen
outside-range bodies, success is 97.28\% and 97.15\%, yet the worst bodies
succeed on only 3.0\% and 13.3\% of those clips. These findings support the
central claim: \emph{one shared controller learns morphology-specific tracking
with strong average transfer to unseen shapes, but uneven robustness across
individual bodies}. The design evidence explains practical trade-offs; it does
not yet isolate the benefit of explicit morphology inputs or establish uniform
held-out-motion performance across all bodies.

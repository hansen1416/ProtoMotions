\section{Discussion and Limitations}\label{sec:discussion}
The central result is a shared controller over a large paired motion/body
corpus. The held-out-motion evaluation shows strong tracking on selected
trained bodies. Unseen-body replay reveals a less uniform outcome: 91.64\%
overall success, with most bodies transferring well and four exhibiting
severe tracking errors. Both results characterize the system. Excluding
those bodies would change the population rather than resolve its limitations.

\paragraph{Coverage and mechanism.}
One body per held-out clip does not measure all-body consistency. Complete
150-by-128 replay concerns training clips; the unseen-body panel contains
only 92 held-out identities. Original/mirrored variants stay together, but
splits are not demonstrated to be subject- or recording-disjoint.
Morphology-input interventions or a matched policy without those inputs are
needed to isolate their benefit: state and references also carry shape
information. Transfer inside $[-3,3]^{10}$ does not establish extrapolation
to arbitrary bodies or different skeletal topologies.

\paragraph{Design trade-offs.}
Repeated runs support slot/type attention as a compromise, not a universal
winner. Historical jerk ordering does not persist across seeds. Refinement
reduces sliding and penetration while raising position jerk; comparisons
against different targets establish neither causal learning benefit nor
absence of cost. The physical analysis awaits a clip-level contrast before
an interaction conclusion is warranted.

\paragraph{Body-specific failures.}
Opposite-template contrasts and static mass measurements identify candidate
mechanisms, not verified defects. The fourth severe case lacks the same mass
pattern. Unseen reference processing also differs from training. Dynamic
traces, rendered comparisons, and controlled interventions are needed to
separate reference, actuation, and controller effects.

\paragraph{Physical and resource scope.}
Estimated masses, collision proxies, and mass-scaled gains are modeling
choices. Clearance correction is not dynamics optimization; success and
jerk do not certify biomechanical realism. The flat-ground task excludes
objects and partners. Caption-derived failure families are descriptions,
not causal diagnoses. The corpus is derived: variants are combinations of
clips and bodies, not independent captures. Caption alignment and
redistribution permissions require auditing before a benchmark-release claim.

\paragraph{Pending checkpoint comparison.}
The full-data polish checkpoint additionally trained on all original motion
splits. Replaying the same unseen pairs with a trained-body control can test
whether further training changes transfer. It cannot replace the original
held-out-motion test or separate training-duration and motion-coverage effects.
No result from that checkpoint is assumed here.

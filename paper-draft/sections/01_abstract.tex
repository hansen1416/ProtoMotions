\begin{abstract}
Physics-based imitation across body shapes requires consistent references
and simulated dynamics. We construct a corpus linking 20,951 AMASS-derived
HumanML3D clip identities to HUMOS-generated references and matching
SMPL-based assets for 128 body configurations, yielding 2,681,728 variants.
Contact-aware refinement reduces sliding and ground penetration with small
pose changes, at the cost of higher reference position jerk. One
morphology-conditioned temporal controller is trained directly on 18,855
clips across all 128 bodies using streamed motion residency. Standalone
original-checkpoint evaluation achieves 98.47\% validation and 99.14\% test
success on 1,048 held-out clips per split, with one selected trained body per
clip. A separate replay of 1,024 clips across 32 unseen bodies achieves
91.64\% overall success: 28 bodies track at 99.74\%, while four have severe
tracking failures with unresolved causes. Three-seed development comparisons
support slot/type attention as a practical trade-off, without a consistent
advantage on all measures. These results establish large-scale shared
imitation and substantial but non-uniform transfer within the sampled body
range; they do not establish all-body held-out-motion coverage or isolate
the benefit of explicit morphology conditioning.
\end{abstract}

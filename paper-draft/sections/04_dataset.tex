\section{Overview}\label{sec:dataset}

A body configuration $b=(g,\boldsymbol{\beta})$ specifies a SMPL template
and shape coefficients. A \emph{clip} denotes a source segment, including a
mirrored segment when present, and a \emph{variant} denotes that clip on one
body configuration. For source clip $c$, HUMOS supplies a shape-specific
reference $\hat{s}_{c,b}(t)$ and SMPLSim supplies the matching asset, so the
corpus connects every clip identity to a motion description, 128 generated
variants, and their matching simulation assets
(Figure~\ref{fig:data_pipeline}). Changing $b$ changes geometry, reference
positions, mass/inertia, and mass-scaled actuation even though topology is
shared, so the task is to track these paired references with one policy rather
than impose identical world-space paths on all bodies.

MorphMimic learns $\pi_\theta(a_t\mid o_t,b)$ from the present state, history,
future reference targets, and body information, with the same parameters
serving every body. Clips can change on reset while each environment retains
its asset, and evaluation preserves reference/body identity. The flat-ground
task has no external objects or partners, and captions are metadata, not
controller input.

\paragraph{Source motion.}
We use the AMASS-derived motion portion of HumanML3D~\cite{amass,humanml3d},
drawn from the 18 source collections listed in
Appendix~\ref{app:preprocessing}. Source SMPL+H sequences are processed at
20\,Hz, with clip extraction, temporal cropping, mirroring, and
treadmill/skating cleanup following the HumanML3D/HUMOS pipeline, so
HumanML3D identities are preserved. The pipeline produced 22,459 candidate clip
identities, counting original and mirrored clips; a text-based filter removed
1,508 that appear to require external objects or environmental support, leaving
20,951 identities for flat-ground imitation. The filter is a task-scope
heuristic and does not prove that every retained reference is support-free or
dynamically feasible. HumanML3D descriptions stay associated with the variants
of their source clip as metadata; their semantic fidelity is not audited
(Appendix~\ref{app:captions}), and they supply keyword-based motion classes only
for the exploratory physical analysis in Appendix~\ref{app:physics}.

\paragraph{Body configurations.}
A body configuration is $b=(g,\boldsymbol{\beta})$, where $g$ identifies the
female or male SMPL variant and $\boldsymbol{\beta}\in\R^{10}$ contains shape
coefficients. We sample
\begin{equation}
    \beta_{ij}\sim\mathcal{U}(-3,3),\qquad
    i=1,\ldots,64,\quad j=1,\ldots,10,
    \label{eq:shape_sampling}
\end{equation}
and cross the 64 vectors with the two variants, giving 128 body configurations
rather than 128 independent coefficient vectors. Uniform sampling is a
controlled source of variation, not a representative sample of human
populations.

\paragraph{Reference and asset generation.}
For each clip, the pretrained HUMOS model~\cite{humos} receives the processed
pose and root features and the target body parameters, and returns a
shape-conditioned reference (root-relative 6D rotations with translation). In
parallel, SMPL bodies~\cite{smpl} with identical parameters are converted to
MJCF assets with SMPLSim~\cite{smplsim}, using mass estimation and hand and foot
simplifications. Each asset has 24 rigid bodies and 69 actuated joint
coordinates, with its own collision proxies, joint ranges, and estimated masses
and inertias; these are simulation-model estimates, not measurements of human
participants.

\paragraph{Reference refinement.}
Generated sequences are then converted to ProtoMotions' Z-up convention and
resampled from 20 to 30\,Hz. Forward kinematics (FK) uses the matching asset
rather than a single neutral skeleton, and the packaged representation stores
global body positions and rotations, local rotations, joint positions,
velocities, and contact labels. Refinement is applied independently to each
variant in five steps; default settings are in
Appendix~\ref{app:preprocessing}. \emph{Contacts:} ankle and toe contacts
are validated, or detected from foot position and velocity when labels are
unusable; short gaps are closed and very short runs removed. These label
candidate stance intervals and are not measured forces. \emph{Rotation
filtering:} joint rotations are filtered with sign-continuous quaternions,
normalization, and a bounded geodesic correction, and contact transitions divide
the sequence into segments so that filtering does not average across a change of
stance. \emph{Root correction:} a conservative least-squares root translation,
anchored at each contact's median foot position, reduces horizontal stance
sliding; it is temporally regularized and capped, so it does not guarantee exact
foot locking and does not solve for new joint angles. \emph{Ground clearance:}
with floor height zero and margin $\epsilon_z$, the lift required at frame $t$ is
\begin{equation}
    \ell_t^{\mathrm{req}}
      = \max\!\left(0,\epsilon_z-\min_{x\in\mathcal{C}_b(t)}x_z\right),
    \label{eq:clearance}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{C}_b(t)$ is the collision geometry of body $b$, and a smoothed
lift envelope stays above this requirement. This prevents penetration of the
checked geometry in the reference; it does not establish balanced support,
realistic impacts, or freedom from self-collision. \emph{Reconstruction:} FK and
velocities are recomputed from the corrected root and rotations within each
motion. The objective is modest, to reduce measurable kinematic artifacts while
preserving the generated motion, and the refinement audit in Section~\ref{sec:refinement_results}
reports artifact and fidelity measures; improved kinematics alone does not
demonstrate improved policy learning or dynamic feasibility.

\paragraph{Scale and splits.}
Each per-clip file stores the 128 variants with frame counts, sampling rates,
and the clip and asset identities needed to recover body parameters, so the
training system can stream clip files without downloading the complete corpus.
Splits are by clip identity: all variants of a clip, and original/mirrored
partners, share a partition (Table~\ref{tab:dataset_counts}), and variants are
generated combinations rather than independent captures. The split is versioned
with seed 42 and roughly 90/5/5 proportions; the 150 development clips used for
design comparisons and the reference audit ($150\times128=19{,}200$ variants)
are reserved within the training split together with their mirrored partners,
286 identities in total, and the partners are not part of the 150-clip analysis
(Appendix~\ref{app:provenance}); the test manifest is never loaded by
the training workflow. The splits test held-out clip identities on the trained
body set. They have not been shown to be disjoint by subject or capture
recording, which limits claims about broader motion generalization.
